Entry 1

Twelve improved lower bounds for constant-weight binary codes

Status

Listed in Andries Brouwer’s table of constant-weight codes, cells marked Ol and credited to Antoine Olivier.

Date

Sent on 10, 19 and 22 October 2026 and added by the maintainer. Three other bounds I sent were accepted, then beaten by other authors (listed below).

Result

A(n, d, w) is the largest number of binary words of length n and weight w with pairwise Hamming distance at least d. Each line gives the new lower bound and the value the table listed when the code was sent.

Data

  • A(26, 8, 11) ≥ 2044 was 2042
  • A(27, 6, 11) ≥ 19344 was 19305
  • A(27, 8, 9) ≥ 1044 was 1029
  • A(27, 8, 11) ≥ 2422 was 2421
  • A(28, 8, 11) ≥ 3791 was 3785
  • A(28, 10, 14) ≥ 817 was 814
  • A(30, 10, 11) ≥ 514 was 512
  • A(30, 10, 13) ≥ 940 was 936
  • A(32, 10, 9) ≥ 307 was 305
  • A(40, 8, 8) ≥ 3687 was 3683

Since beaten: A(29, 8, 8) ≥ 1062 (now 1064, Hu), A(33, 8, 8) ≥ 1806 (now 1852, Lin), A(37, 8, 8) ≥ 2846 (now 2860, Lin).

Code files and checking programs: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23776838.

Method

Each code starts from the table’s own code for the same cell and enlarges it. An integer linear program swaps a group of neighbouring words for a group one word larger; for A(27, 6, 11), 39 words could be added to the table’s code without removing any.

Two programs written separately check length, weight, distinct words and every pairwise distance in exact arithmetic. One was written with Claude, the other by DeepSeek V4.1 Flash from the problem statement alone. Both accepted all the files on 29 October 2026.

Entry 2

Five spherical codes in dimensions 11 to 14

Status

Listed in Henry Cohn’s table of spherical codes, each row credited Antoine Olivier, 2026.

Date

Ten codes were submitted on 19 October 2026; these five are still listed. The other five have since been improved by others (listed below).

Result

A spherical code is a set of N points on the unit sphere in dimension d; the goal is to keep the largest cosine between any two points as small as possible. For these five (d, N) pairs, the codes improved on the table values read on 19 October 2026, before submission.

Since improved: (13, 72) by Antoine Jay; (13, 95), (15, 75), (17, 83) and (17, 84) by Henry Cohn. All ten files and both checking programs: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23776897.

Method

Local polishing by sequential linear programming. Most codes start from entries the table marked as needing more optimization; (12, 92) starts from Ilmari Vahteristo’s 93-point code with one point removed.

Each coordinate is an integer divided by 252, so the largest cosine can be checked exactly. A checker written with Claude and an independent one by DeepSeek V4.1 Flash both confirmed every value. Whether any of these codes is optimal remains open.

Entry 3

Six atomic clusters below the Cambridge Cluster Database

Status

Sent on 22 October 2026 to Jonathan Doye and David Wales, who maintain the Cambridge Cluster Database. No reply yet; the database pages are unchanged.

Result

For two model potentials, the modified Dzugutov potential (mDz) and Z1, the database lists the lowest energy known for each number of atoms N. These six configurations have a lower energy than the listed one. Whether they are global minima is open.

Coordinates and checking programs: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23775350.

Method

Basin-hopping, seeded from my best configurations with one atom more or one atom less. Interval arithmetic encloses the energy of each configuration and of the database’s own file: each new energy lies strictly below the listed one. A second checker written by DeepSeek V4.1 Flash agreed on all six.

Entry 4

Periods for five variable stars missing from VSX

Status

Data archive published on Zenodo on 29 October 2026: doi:10.5281/zenodo.23534347. Not yet submitted to VSX; a note for the Research Notes of the AAS is in preparation.

Result

A search of TESS light curves of 517 high-latitude stars found five periodic variables absent from the AAVSO International Variable Star Index (VSX). Three of them have no period or type in SIMBAD or VizieR either, apart from an automated fit at twice the period for TIC 243167066 (Green et al. 2023, not kept as an ellipsoidal candidate); Maíz Apellániz et al. (2023) flag all three as variable from Gaia scatter alone.

The other two were reported before. For TIC 436556517, timed eclipses give 2.2056 days, where Thiemann et al. (2021) list 0.44 days and Green et al. (2023) an automated fit at 2.2007 days, not kept as an ellipsoidal candidate; folded on that fit, the eclipses drift by hours. TIC 402964191 was scored by Green et al. (2023) but not kept as an ellipsoidal candidate either, and TESS shows a stable 0.25473-day term.

Data

VSX type codes: BY, BY Draconis (spotted dwarf); ROT, spotted rotating star; EA, Algol-type eclipsing binary; VAR, variable of unspecified type.

StarTypePeriod (days)
TIC 105616985BY0.6834 ± 0.0004
TIC 243167163BY0.4930 ± 0.0004
TIC 243167066ROT2.30 ± 0.09
TIC 436556517EA2.2056294 ± 0.0000024
TIC 402964191VAR0.25473 ± 0.00005, or 0.50945 if ellipsoidal
Method

TIC 105616985: period from seven years of ZTF photometry, confirmed in four TESS sectors. TIC 243167163: found in three sectors of TESS light curves processed by the TESS Science Processing Operations Center (TESS-SPOC), 0.4928 to 0.4932 days; the adopted value comes from ZTF, where the signal is weak. TIC 243167066 and TIC 402964191: TESS-SPOC, with the same period in the Quick-Look Pipeline (QLP) light curves, built from the same TESS images. TIC 436556517: 28 timed eclipses in three TESS sectors from 2019 to 2024.

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash wrote its own period search without seeing my periods. It was run, with a one-line fix for a newer NumPy, on the same data on 29 October 2026; the archive reports where it agrees and where it does not.

Entry 5

Five new terms in two OEIS sequences

Status

a(9) = 249696 and a(10) = 11498629 of A277203, approved by the OEIS editors on 27 October 2026.

a(8) = 9819, a(9) = 237641 and a(10) = 11236576 of A321911, approved on 28 October 2026.

Date

Sent on 20 October 2026.

Result

A277203 counts the distinct chromatic symmetric functions of graphs on n vertices; A321911 counts them for connected graphs. The sequences previously stopped at n = 8 and n = 7.

Method

Two graphs have the same chromatic symmetric function exactly when they have the same number of stable partitions of each type. Every graph on n vertices was generated, with nauty’s geng and with a second generator, and the distinct type vectors were counted. Two exact programs agreed on every term.

AI
The enumeration programs were written with Claude (Anthropic) under my direction.

κ bounds

For the community project CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds, which improves the exponent κ in a conditional O(n (log n)1−κ) bound for integer multiplication, I reran selected verification programs from four pull requests at recorded commits. Checks for #207 and #197 and a diagnostic for #199 reproduced; for #202, one check passed and another failed on a hash mismatch. I reported the reruns in pull request #214, closed without merging on 14 October 2026; it is credited in CONTRIBUTORS.md. The reruns cover the programs only and give no new κ value.

Exoplanets

In the eumemic exoplanet catalogue, the TRICERATOPS runs used a wrong input for TIC 160420269. I reported it on 15 and 18 October 2026; the author fixed the catalogue, and 55 signals changed tier.
